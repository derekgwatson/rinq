Derek: *"I'm getting reports from a few staff now that voicemails in Tina aren't getting the
transcription attached."*

They weren't, and hadn't been for eight days. The OpenAI account Whisper runs on had run out of
credit at **2026-09-10 ~01:20 UTC** (~11:20 Sydney). Every voicemail since then — **254** of them —
was filed with no transcription and a ticket line reading *"(Transcription pending or
unavailable)"*.

Two PRs merged and deployed (#15, #16), and the whole backlog transcribed and posted to its
tickets.

## Why it was invisible — the shape worth remembering

`_emit_voicemail_record` omits Twilio's `transcribe="true"` whenever `OPENAI_API_KEY` is set. Twilio
will only transcribe a recording if that attribute was on the `<Record>` verb **before the caller
spoke**, so once Whisper is on there is no second source — a failure is not a downgrade to lower
quality, it is *no transcription, permanently*.

And `is_configured` only asks whether a key is **present**. That is exactly the question that stays
`True` when the account runs dry. Three independent things then had to be true for eight days of
silence:

1. the failure produced no alert;
2. the ticket wording (*"pending or unavailable"*) was indistinguishable from one mumbled caller;
3. `raise_for_status()` discarded the response body, so the log said `429` and not *"no credits"*.

Written up as **gotcha 40** in `CLAUDE.md`.

## What shipped (1) — say why, and raise it (PR #15, `fc0afc7`)

- `WhisperService.transcribe` returns a `TranscriptionResult(text, error, fault)` instead of a bare
  `Optional[str]`. `error` is a plain sentence fragment written for a staff member — *"the
  transcription account has run out of credit"* — not a status code.
- **`fault` separates "we are broken" from "there was nothing to transcribe."** A silent voicemail
  must never raise an alarm, or the alarm stops meaning anything.
- The voicemail ticket/email now names the cause and says to play the recording.
- `services/transcription_alerts.py` emails the **tenant admins** (derived from
  `tenant_users.role='admin'`, so there is no recipient list to maintain — today Derek and Clare).
  Deduped on the reason with a 6-hour cooldown: a service that fails, works, then fails again every
  few minutes costs one email, not 254. A *different* reason always gets through. A success marks
  the outage resolved but leaves the timestamp, so the cooldown still applies — clearing it outright
  is what would let a flap send one email per voicemail.
- `scripts/backfill_voicemail_transcriptions.py` — dry run by default, resumable, `--commit` to
  apply. Recovery is only possible because **nothing deletes a voicemail from Twilio until a
  transcription lands**; the audio was all still there. `db.get_untranscribed_voicemails(since)`
  added alongside its siblings rather than reaching into `_get_conn` from a script.

Failure-reason mapping was checked against real OpenAI error bodies (credit exhausted, rate limit,
bad key, 5xx, unparseable) and the alert dedup/cooldown/flap behaviour against a fake DB and mailer.

## What shipped (2) — don't quit over one slow request (PR #16, `c8092e0`)

The first backfill pass stopped at **171 of 254**. The abort-on-fault rule was meant to stop a
no-credit run burning through 250 rows printing the same line; it also fired on a single 60-second
timeout, which is not the same thing. Only a **streak** means the service is down — three in a row
now aborts, and any success resets the counter. Found by running the thing, not by reading it.

## Result

Second pass finished the rest. **254 transcribed and posted** to their existing Zelda tickets, each
carrying a line explaining it was added late so an old ticket lighting up makes sense.

Six could not be, and none is a loss:

- **5 were genuinely silent** — 2 to 14 seconds, someone hanging up on the beep.
- **1 was 313 bytes** (ticket #710451) — a valid MP3 header and essentially nothing else.

Two further rows had no ticket at all: both zero seconds, so nothing was ever raised.

## Notable

- **A dependency on a personal billing account.** Voicemail transcription for Watson runs on
  Derek's own OpenAI account. Auto-reload covers the obvious failure; a closed account or rotated
  key would not be covered. Flagged to Derek, not actioned.
- **Spend is trivial** — ~240 voicemail-minutes/month, about **$1.50**; the entire 8-day backlog
  cost ~40c. A large bill on that account means something other than voicemail is using it.
- `known_issues` already carried *"Whisper hallucinates on silence (e.g. 'Thanks for watching')"*.
  The new `fault=False` path treats an empty transcript as "nothing said" and leaves the row
  untouched, which is the right handling for that too.
- The heartbeat described Whisper's fallback as *"Twilio transcribeCallback when no key"*. True at
  **startup**, and the reason this outage was invisible — there is no fallback once a recording
  exists. Corrected in this pass.
